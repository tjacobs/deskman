A robot you can hack

Deskman isn't a sealed consumer assistant — it's designed to be modified. The software runs on Linux and Python, and the hardware is accessible, so you can change how it behaves, replace models, modify the motion system, add sensors, or use it as the starting point for your own robot. The body is 3D printed, so it's easy to remix mechanically too.

Movement

Three actuated degrees of freedom on Waveshare bus servos. The head moves in pitch and yaw to turn toward people and react during conversation. A third actuator moves the hat, covering and revealing the camera and display. Drive it all from your own Python.

Vision

An Arducam camera gives Deskman sight — face tracking (out of the box), person and object detection, and visual interaction. The software pairs vision with the actuated head, so Deskman physically orients toward the people it sees.

Software

Open. Yours to change.

Runs on Linux and Python, with CUDA accelerating speech-to-text, the LLM, and text-to-speech. Compatible with modern open-source STT, TTS, computer vision, and local language models — swap components as new models arrive. You're not locked into a proprietary AI service.

Why a physical robot?

Talking to AI on a screen is useful. Talking to a robot sitting on your desk feels different. Deskman gives AI a physical presence — it can turn toward you, make eye contact, speak naturally, and serve as a platform for experimenting with embodied AI and human-robot interaction.
